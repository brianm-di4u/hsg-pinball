## 1. Coin-op, slot machines and pinball in Hot Springs and Arkansas

### Arkansas law on pinball (was it banned?)
- In 1930 the Arkansas Supreme Court held that a Mills Novelty "Baseball Mint Vender" was a gambling device. For a nickel it gave mints plus a chance number of slugs for a baseball game. The court counted free play as a "thing of value" (*Rankin v. Mills Novelty Co.*, Oct. 27, 1930, Pulaski County). https://cap-redacted-demo.lil.tools/ark/182/cases/0561-01.json | documented
- In 1934 the court held that coin-operated **marble machines** (the early pinball type) were gambling devices **per se**. Each nickel released 10 marbles, which the player shot with levers onto a numbered board, and the high score won a prize. The court affirmed an order to destroy machines seized from a Pine Bluff cigar store and sandwich shop. It held this even though the machines were sometimes run "for amusement only" (*Steed v. State*, 189 Ark. 389, June 11, 1934). https://cap-redacted-demo.lil.tools/ark/189/cases/0389-01.json | documented
- In 1937 the court applied *Steed* to "Home Run," a 5-cent electric baseball marble machine in a Little Rock barber shop, and held it a gambling device (*Stanley v. State*, 194 Ark. 483, July 12, 1937). https://cite.case.law/ark/194/483/ | documented (snippet)
- The state never banned flipper pinball outright. Instead, the free-game feature was legalized by statute: Act 137 of 1933, as amended by Act 201 of 1939, codified as Ark. Stat. § 84-2611. In 1966 the court held that free games alone are lawful, but a pinball machine whose free games were paid off in cash (a nickel a game, at a Little Rock cafe) became a gaming device (*Bostic v. City of Little Rock*, 241 Ark. 671, Dec. 19, 1966). https://cap-redacted-demo.lil.tools/ark/241/cases/0671-01.json | documented
- **Takeaway for design:** in the 1930s Arkansas courts treated marble and pinball machines as gambling devices. From 1933/1939 the law allowed free-replay pinball, and enforcement targeted payoffs. Unlike New York City (banned 1942–1976), no statewide or Hot Springs ban on pinball was found. | likely (inferred from the above)
- Current law, Ark. Code § 5-66-111, outlaws multi-coin "bingo-type" pinball machines that add odds or extra free games. Its stated intent is to prohibit "so-called 'bingo'-type pinball machines," and it cites the federal shipping ban in 15 U.S.C. § 1172. https://codes.findlaw.com/ar/title-5-criminal-offenses/ar-code-sect-5-66-111/ | documented
- A 2023 Attorney General opinion (No. 2023-008) still splits devices into gambling devices per se (*Steed*) and devices made illegal by use (*Bostic*). https://ezel.ai/ag-opinions/ar/2023-0008-q1-does-arkansas-code-annotated-26-57-401-exempt-amusement-d | likely (snippet; secondary host)
- National context: New York City banned pinball in 1942 and lifted the ban in April 1976. Other big-city bans included Chicago, Los Angeles, Boston, New Orleans and Philadelphia. https://www.history.com/articles/that-time-america-outlawed-pinball | documented (secondary, snippet)
- **False lead:** a 1981 "News-Record" story about a pinball and video gameroom ordinance in "Hot Springs" comes from the DigitalNC archive. It is Madison County, **North Carolina** (Hot Springs, NC), not Arkansas, so do not use it. https://newspapers.digitalnc.org/lccn/sn92074087/1981-11-26/ed-1/seq-1/ | documented (that it is NC)

### Slot machines in the illegal-casino era (additions to `gangster-era-and-gambling.md`)
- Spa Amusement Co. supplied slot machines to the Vapors, the Belvedere Country Club and other Hot Springs casinos. Dane Harris was an owner, and Ernie Bone ran it. It closed for a few months in 1964 and reopened in March 1965. https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2017/oct/07/the-slot-machine-man-20171007/ | documented (ADG 2017, full read)
- Hot Springs slots "featured five ways to hit a jackpot" and came in nickel, dime and quarter versions (ADG, citing Threadgill's book). Some carried stickers reading "this machine made by Spa Amusement." Same URL | documented
- The Old State House Museum holds a Hot Springs slot machine: a Mills Novelty Co. (Chicago) 5-cent machine with a 1910 design copyright, serial 338046, catalogued c. 1950 (object 2009.117.06). Each nickel paid out a piece of gum plus a play credit, a ploy to pass as a vending machine. It was confiscated from an unnamed Hot Springs gambling hall. https://collections.oldstatehouse.com/objects/20244/slot-machine-from-hot-springs-arkansas | documented
- On Feb. 29, 1956, State Police raided two Hot Springs clubs, arrested 17 people and seized gambling equipment from the Southern Club and the Pine Supper Club. https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/?p=15745 | documented (EOA "This Day")
- The Jan. 4, 1963 bombing of the Vapors exposed "slot machines and craps tables" to the street. The Belvedere was bombed March 11, 1959. https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2019/mar/17/hot-springs-notorious-casinos-20190317/ | documented
- The Southern Club building became Josephine Tussaud's Wax Museum after E. L. Lane bought it in 1971. https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/southern-club-1208/ | documented
- No source was found for a historic pinball parlor or penny arcade on Central Avenue in any era. The one documented pre-1960 amusement district is Whittington Park (merry-go-rounds, roller coasters), already in the corpus. | gap
- Modern arcade (setting only): Funtrackers Family Fun Center, 2614 Albert Pike Rd., is listed for modern videogames as of Jan. 2025. https://arcade-museum.com/location/Arkansas/Funtrackers-Family-Fun-Center-Hot-Springs-2970 | likely

### The 1967 raids and the destruction of machines
- On Aug. 17, 1967, State Police director Lynn Davis brought 20 troopers and seized equipment from the Bridge Street, Citizens, Ohio and White Front clubs. None were operating, and the operators had been tipped off. https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2017/oct/07/the-slot-machine-man-20171007/ | documented (the corpus has the clubs; the 20 troopers and the tip-off are new)
- In October 1967, troopers took 111 slots from Southern Club manager Harry Columbus's Bellaire Drive home. The same day they raided a second slot repair shop on Westbrook Street, and "more than 100" machines were taken across the two raids. Same URL | documented (Westbrook St. shop is new)
- **Destruction:** seized machines were taken to a rock quarry, run over by a bulldozer, then burned (no date or quarry name given). Same URL | documented
- An Old State House Museum photo (object 2008.085.03) records that Col. Davis and the State Police "hauled the evidence to a gravel pit for its destruction" after the October 1967 raids seized "hundreds of slot machines." https://collections.oldstatehouse.com/objects/16347/photograph-arkansas-state-police--hot-springs-gambling-rai | documented
- A second photo (object 2008.085.02) shows Capt. Bill Miller, Maj. Melven DeLong and Sgt. Jim Tyler "destroying gambling equipment" from a Hot Springs raid in 1967. https://collections.oldstatehouse.com/objects/16346/photograph-arkansas-state-police--burning-gambling-items | documented
- EOA: "gambling devices were destroyed in large numbers," which broke the illegal trade. https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/southern-club-1208/ | documented
- A popular blog claims the machines were burned **publicly**. That is not supported by the primary sources, which place the destruction at a quarry or gravel pit. https://allaroundarkansas.blogspot.com/2022/06/all-around-arkansas-gambling-and-spa.html | legend/unverified
- One source says Tony Frazier saw an FBI raid on the slot company in 1967 (EOA calls it "Spa City Amusement Co.," ADG "Spa Amusement"). https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/history-of-hot-springs-gambling-museum-15477/ | documented (name conflict)
- The ADG 2017 article counted 65 machines at the Gambling Museum, while EOA says 80 functional (15 used in Hot Springs). Keep both counts as a dated conflict. | documented

## 3. Hot Springs Mountain Tower (additions to the corpus)
- The tower has two decks. The **lower deck is enclosed and air conditioned, with exhibits**; the **upper deck is open-air** at 1,256 ft above sea level. Visitors ride an elevator. https://www.nps.gov/places/hot-springs-mountain-tower.htm | documented
- The tower is run as a park concession. The contract was signed March 1982, and it opened in June 1983 at 216 ft (the corpus already has the height and dates). Same URL | documented
- **View: "140 square miles"** of countryside (NPS tower page, EOA). Another NPS page says "140 miles," and Wikipedia says "120 mile view" (a c. 1924 Rix Tower advertisement). For the Rix Tower, Supt. Scott Smith estimated a 30–40 mile radius. Use "140 square miles" as the official figure. https://www.nps.gov/places/hot-springs-mountain-tower.htm ; https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/hot-springs-887/ ; https://home.nps.gov/hosp/planyourvisit/outdooractivities.htm | documented (conflict: unit mix-up)
- EOA dates the opening to 1983, which agrees with NPS. This favors 1983 over Wikipedia's 1982 in the corpus conflict, though 1982 is the construction-start year. | documented

## 4. Thermal water (NPS and USGS)
- **47** hot springs at an **average 143°F**. https://www.nps.gov/hosp/upload/Common-Frequently-Asked-Questions-v3.docx (NPS FAQ) | documented
- The park "goes through about **700,000 gallons** of water per day" (NPS FAQ). The Display Spring page says the roughly four dozen springs "produce over 700,000 gallons of water a day, at an average temperature of 143 degrees." https://www.nps.gov/places/maurice-historic-display-spring.htm | documented
- USGS: the water surfaces at about 143°F; it was rain "over 4,000 years ago"; it circulates to depths of **2,000–8,000 ft**; there are **47 spring boxes, 35 monitored** by a hydrologic technician; the park can distribute **over 600,000 gal/day** for public use, routed to a reservoir under Bathhouse Row. https://www.usgs.gov/geology-and-ecology-of-national-parks/hydrology-hot-springs-national-park | documented
- NPS "Experience the Water" page: the water "comes out of the ground at 143 degrees F"; it is "over 4,000-year-old spring water"; the park has 7 thermal fountains and 2 cold fountains (Happy Hollow, Whittington); there are 6 places to touch the thermal water. https://www.nps.gov/hosp/planyourvisit/thermal-springs.htm | documented
- Heating mechanism (NPS FAQ): the springs are not volcanic. The water warms by the geothermal gradient, about **4°F per 300 ft** of descent. Organisms found in the water include ostracods, blue-green algae and nano-bacteria. Park area is 5,549.1 acres; there are 26 miles of trails. NPS FAQ URL above | documented
- The scientific age figure remains about 4,400 years (USGS PP 1044-C, already in the corpus). NPS rounds it to "over 4,000." Game copy: "47 springs · 143°F · ~700,000 gal/day · 4,000+ years." | documented

## 5. Duck tours (setting only, no logos)
- National Park Duck Tours runs WWII **DUKW** amphibious vehicles from **418 Central Ave.** (across from Bathhouse Row and the Buckstaff). The route goes south on Hwy 7 to **Lake Hamilton**, circles St. John's Island, then returns. Tours run about 75–90 minutes. https://www.hotsprings.org/places/activities/national-park-duck-tours/ ; https://www.rideaduck.com/ | documented (promo)
- In 2015 the company ran about 12 DUKWs, with about 20 more kept for spare parts. https://www.arkansasonline.com/news/2015/sep/03/wisequacks-set-tone-of-hot-springs-duck/ | documented
- **Founding year: not found** on the company site, the tourism sites or the ADG. The company calls itself "one of the oldest" tours of the park. The owner in 2018 was Stacy Roberts. https://www.pressreader.com/usa/the-sentinel-record/20180721/281526521841564 | gap / likely (snippet)
- **Sensitive history:** on **May 1, 1999**, the DUKW *Miss Majestic*, operated by a **different** company (Land and Lakes Tours, Inc.), sank on Lake Hamilton about 7 minutes into the lake leg. **13 of 20 passengers died, including 3 children.** The NTSB blamed inadequate maintenance; contributing factors were weak Coast Guard oversight and a canopy roof that trapped passengers (NTSB/MAR-02/01). Never use a sinking or duck-boat peril as a gameplay hazard or joke. https://www.ntsb.gov/investigations/Pages/DCA99MM021.aspx ; https://professionalmariner.com/ntsb-recommends-adding-reserve-buoyancy-to-amphibious-craft/ | documented
- DUKW background: designed in 1942 (GMC code D-U-K-W); 21,147 were built by 1945. https://www.rideaduck.com/history | likely (operator page)

## 6. Magic Springs (only what the corpus lacks)
- EOA names the developer as **Leisure and Recreation Concepts (LARC) of Dallas**. In the mid-1980s **Nygem Company** (controlling interest held by Melvyn Bell of Little Rock) took over. PARC Management of Jacksonville **bought** the park in 2008 and ran it until 2016. https://encyclopediaofarkansas.net/entries/magic-springs-5794/ | documented (the corpus cites EOA only via a search summary)
- EOA calls the Arkansas Twister a "wooden coaster built in 1978 and retired in 2025." The 1978 build year is the coaster's original construction elsewhere (corpus: bought used in 1991, opened at Magic Springs 1992). This agrees with the corpus's 2025 retirement value. Same URL | documented (nuance)
- EOA ride list adds names the corpus may lack: Diamond Mine Run, The Hawk, Brain Drain, Plummet Summit, Rum Runner Pirate Ship, Old No. 2 Logging Company, Ozark Mountain Taxi Company, Razorback Roundup Bumper Cars and a kiddie set. **Do not reuse these names in game.** Same URL | documented

## 9. Bathhouse Row (corrections and additions only)
- The NPS FAQ open/close table: Hale opened 1893, closed 1978; Maurice 1911–1974; Buckstaff 1912 to present; Fordyce opened March 1, 1915, closed 1962; Superior 1916–1983; Ozark 1922–1977; Quapaw 1922–1984; Lamar 1923–1985. https://www.nps.gov/hosp/upload/Common-Frequently-Asked-Questions-v3.docx | documented
  - This adds an **1893 opening for the Hale** next to the corpus's 1892–93 build, and a **1911 opening for the Maurice** next to the corpus's "begun 1911, finished 1912." Record both as opening vs completion.
- NPS FAQ current uses: Superior brewery; Hale "boutique restaurant / hotel"; Maurice vacant; Fordyce museum and visitor center; Quapaw operating bathhouse; Ozark "art museum" (the cultural center); Buckstaff operating bathhouse; Lamar bookstore (park store). Undated FAQ, about 2020. Same URL | documented
- Only Quapaw (ages 14+) and Buckstaff (ages 10+) offer full thermal-water immersion. https://www.nps.gov/hosp/planyourvisit/thermal-springs.htm | documented
- Fordyce details (NPS FAQ): the marble is from Italy; the fountains were made by American Encaustic Tile Co. of Zanesville, OH, and installed by Southwestern Tile Co. of Little Rock; the stained glass is about 98% original. FDR visited in 1936 but bathed at Warm Springs, GA. The FAQ credits the glass to "Condie-Neale" of St. Louis, which adds to the corpus's Condie-Bray / Condi-Neal conflict. Same URL | documented (conflict noted)
- Maurice: ground broken August 2025 on a $31.6M Great American Outdoors Act rehabilitation (foundation, windows, roof, plumbing, fire suppression, electrical). The aim is to lease and reopen it. **No lessee or completion date found as of 2026-11-01.** https://www.nps.gov/hosp/learn/news/maurice-gaoa-groundbreaking.htm ; https://www.nationalparkstraveler.org/2025/08/113-year-old-bathhouse-being-restored-hot-springs-national-park | documented

## 10. Existing Hot Springs–themed pinball and arcade games
- **"Hot Springs" by D. Gottlieb & Co., 1937**: a single-player electromechanical pinball (KLOV/MOG #11772; IPSND dates it 5/1937). It appears in the January 1940 *Coin Machine Journal* list of 1931–39 games. It was released the same year as Gottlieb's horse-race titles (*Daily Races*, *Multiple Races*), so it may be a racing or payout theme, but this is **unconfirmed**. No backglass description or surviving examples are listed. https://arcade-museum.com/Pinball/hot-springs | documented (existence); theme unknown
  - **Design implication:** do not title the table just "Hot Springs." Avoid a 1937-style horse-race or one-ball payout layout as the core identity. "Spa City Pinball" does not collide with it.
- No Bally, Williams, Stern, Mills or video-arcade title themed on Hot Springs or Arkansas was found. A pinball for Hot Springs, **South Dakota** (also called "Carlsbad of America") would be a different place in any case. | likely (absence of evidence)
